YuCheng Bi is broadly interested in organismal and ecological biology, with a particular focus on how behavior, physiology and species interactions shape the lives of animals. He is drawn to how behaviour and physiological traits of insects are adapted to and regulated by their environment and their interactions with other species. In vertebrates, he is more interested in how unusual structures at the molecular and cellular level carry implications for practical medicine. More broadly, he is fascinated by how species interactions organize communities and shape coexistence.

His current undergraduate project addresses the global patterns of co-extinction risk for mutualist networks. Combining network analysis with large-scale ecological data, he is exploring the factors that determine which species become coextinction champions — those whose loss propagates most widely through the network. In the future, he hopes to link individual-level physiology and behavior with community-level processes, so as to understand how ecological systems respond to global change.

YuCheng is currently a second-year undergraduate in the Biological Sciences Top-Notch Talent Program at Nanjing University (南京大学). He is learning broadly in Zoology, Physiology, molecular Biology, Ecology and Evolutionary Biology and developing undergraduate research projects at our lab. Outside the lab, he enjoys badminton, fitness training and playing the guitar.
